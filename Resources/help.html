FeM

A 6-operator FM synthesizer — reference for every section.

Each voice is one six-operator FM engine, then a resonant low-pass filter and a chorus:

FM Engine → Low-Pass Filter → Chorus → Master

There is no separate amp envelope: the carriers' own envelopes shape each note's loudness, as on classic FM synths. Pitch bend moves the engine; channel pressure (aftertouch) opens the filter.

FM Engine

Six sine-based operators. An algorithm decides which operators modulate which: a carrier is heard, a modulator bends a carrier's phase to add harmonics. Each operator row's caption shows its role in the current algorithm: out (a carrier, shown in green) or mod N (a modulator N steps away from the output).

Algorithm
Operator routing — the 32 classic six-operator algorithms, numbered 1–32. The menu lists each one's carriers (out 1,3); every other operator is a modulator. Low numbers are deep stacks (bright, complex); high numbers are mostly carriers (organ-like, additive; 32 is all six in parallel). Levels are balanced by the number of carriers, so switching algorithm doesn't jump the volume.
Feedback
Sits under the algorithm because the algorithm decides where it goes: one operator per algorithm modulates itself (in algorithms 4 and 6 a small loop of operators) — from a brighter sine toward a buzzy, saw-like tone.
Pitch / Fine
Transpose the whole engine in semitones / cents.
Detune
Stereo detune: left and right are tuned apart by up to 50 cents in total, for width and movement.

Operators

Each operator row:

Wave
Sine, or a small harmonic stack (1+2, 1+3, 1+3+5, 1-4) for richer modulators and carriers.
Ratio
Operator frequency as a multiple of the note. A plain drag (or the mouse wheel) steps through the harmonic ratios 0.5, 1, 2 … 16, which give clean, pitched tones. Shift-drag moves the ratio smoothly instead (hold Ctrl too for very fine steps; Shift + wheel nudges it): small offsets make slow beating, larger in-between ratios such as 1.41 or 3.5 give bells and metallic sounds. Off-grid ratios show three decimals. Double-click snaps back to the nearest harmonic ratio; double-click again for 1. (A fine-tune offset in cents, from older presets or automation, shows as e.g. +7c and is cleared by double-click.)
Level
For a carrier, its loudness. For a modulator, the modulation index — how many harmonics it adds.
Envelope
The operator's own envelope, drawn as a graph. On a carrier it is the loudness contour; on a modulator it shapes the timbre over time (e.g. a bright attack that mellows). A voice is freed once its carriers have faded out.

Envelope graphs (operators and the filter envelope) are edited by dragging their points:

Delay
(operators only) the end of the flat start — drag sideways to make the operator come in late. Dimmed while it is zero.
Attack
the peak — drag sideways.
Decay / Sustain
the corner into the held part — sideways sets the decay time, up/down the sustain level.
Release
the end — drag sideways.

Hold Ctrl while dragging for fine changes. Shift-click or double-click a point to reset it. Hovering or dragging shows the point's value. The time axis is compressed so very short and very long stages both fit; the flat part in the middle stands for the held note. Decay and Release are the time to fade by 60 dB.

Low-Pass Filter + Filter Envelope

Cutoff
Filter frequency. Everything above it is rolled off (24 dB/octave).
Resonance
Emphasis at the cutoff.
Env Amount
How far (in octaves) the Filter Envelope moves the cutoff; negative values sweep it down.
Pressure
How far (in octaves) channel pressure / aftertouch moves the cutoff.
Filter Envelope
Triggered by each note; a graph edited like the operator envelopes (above), without the Delay point.

Response

How note velocity sets each note's loudness.

Vel Floor
The loudness of the softest possible note, as a share of the loudest: 0% (full velocity range) up to 75%; Fixed ignores velocity entirely.
Vel Curve
How loudness grows between the floor and full: Concave gets loud early (light touch), Linear is even, Convex saves the loudest levels for hard playing (heavy touch).

Chorus

Mix
Dry / wet balance.
Rate
Speed of the chorus sweep. One LFO drives every voice, so all notes sweep together; left and right run 90° apart for width.
Depth
How far the delay sweeps (milliseconds).
Delay
Base delay time — shorter is tighter, longer is lusher.
Feedback
Feeds the chorus back into itself for a more resonant, flanger-like colour.

Global

Voices
Poly (10 voices) or Mono (legato: a note played while another is held changes pitch without re-attacking the envelopes).
Bend Range
Pitch-bend range, up to 4 semitones each way. Off ignores pitch bend.
Quality
Normal / High / Best: runs the FM engine at 1× / 2× / 4× the sample rate, reducing harsh aliasing on bright, high-modulation sounds at extra CPU cost.
Master
Output level.
